IS -K ACTUALLY FASTER THAN -J?  WALL TIME, PAIRED OVER FIXTURES
================================================================

  python3 eval/jorder_speed.py --schedule S --length L --fixtures 24 --reps 3

  -f -l wehrmacht -c, -R 1024, 10-pair board, rotor key given, authentic HG
  Nord decrypts.  Single-threaded on an idle box.  Each fixture draws its own
  message, key and board; the arms are -J, -K, and -J AGAIN as a control.
  Startup subtracted per fixture and per arm via an -R 0 run.

VERDICT: yes, everywhere measured, and the saving GROWS WITH LENGTH.  All six
cells clear their own control by an order of magnitude.


THE NUMBERS
-----------

  schedule  L     -K / -J      95% CI            control   plugboards said
  f10       60    -12.9%   [-13.7, -12.1]        -0.1%     -25.1%
  f10      100    -15.8%   [-16.8, -14.8]        -0.2%     -25.1%
  m4f10    100     -8.9%   [ -9.7,  -8.0]        +0.1%     -11.5%
  i4f10    100     -8.6%   [-10.0,  -7.1]        -0.3%     -11.1%
  k4f10    100     -8.2%   [ -9.6,  -6.8]        -0.1%     -11.0%
  k4f10     60     -4.1%   [ -5.0,  -3.2]        +0.8%     -11.0%

The three schedules with a low-order pre-pass are indistinguishable from each
other at L=100 (-8.2, -8.6, -8.9, CIs all overlapping), which is the expected
shape: they replace the same f10 TARGET stage's scans and differ only in a
pre-pass that was already O(26).  f10 replaces every scan in the run and comes
out at roughly double.


THE COUNTER OVERSTATES, AND BY MORE AT SHORT LENGTHS
-----------------------------------------------------

  schedule  L     wall     plugboards   overstatement
  f10       60    -12.9%     -25.1%        1.9x
  f10      100    -15.8%     -25.1%        1.6x
  k4f10     60     -4.1%     -11.0%        2.7x
  k4f10    100     -8.2%     -11.0%        1.3x

score_iter counts calls through the fused score loop.  What -K REMOVES is
counted (325 full decodes per restart, O(L) each); what it ADDS is not (325
O(26) column deltas, plus one cooc_build).  The removed work is linear in L
and the added work is nearly flat, so the net saving grows with L and the
overstatement shrinks -- 2.7x at L=60 falling to 1.3x at L=100 on k4f10.

That length dependence is the useful part: it says the counter is not a fixed
multiple of the truth, so it cannot be corrected by a constant.  Time it.


THE SPREAD IS REAL, BUT SMALL
------------------------------

  cell            -K/-J sd    control sd
  f10      L=60     0.0203      0.0125
  f10      L=100    0.0252      0.0198
  m4f10    L=100    0.0213      0.0218
  i4f10    L=100    0.0358      0.0158
  k4f10    L=100    0.0343      0.0203
  k4f10    L=60     0.0227      0.0191

The two arms visit moves in different orders, so they converge in different
numbers of moves, and how many depends on the message and key -- the
per-fixture ratio is a genuine distribution, not a constant measured with
error.  The control arm is what separates that from measurement noise, and the
excess is there in four of six cells (largest on i4f10 and k4f10 at L=100,
0.036 and 0.034 against controls of 0.016 and 0.020).

But it is nowhere near large enough to stop a mean being quoted.  At 24
fixtures every CI is under +/-1.5pp.


WHAT THIS REPLACES, AND WHY THE OLD ANSWER WAS WRONG
------------------------------------------------------

An earlier two-fixture probe reported k4f10 at L=100 as -3.3% on one fixture
and -19.7% on the other, and that spread was written up as the ratio being
trajectory-dependent to the point of being unquotable.

IT WAS A BROKEN FIXTURE, NOT TRAJECTORY.  The probe's second fixture
enciphered with -g DKR and then climbed with -g QMW: it timed a WRONG-KEY
climb, which converges quite differently.  All four of its readings are void.

The first fixture's readings survive and agree with this run where they were
repeated -- f10 L=100 read -15.1% three times against -15.8% here -- and
scatter where they were read once (k4f10 L=100 -3.3% against -8.2%), which is
what a single fixture drawn from an sd-0.034 distribution does.

Two lessons, one specific and one general.  The specific one: a timing probe
that varies the ciphertext must vary the key it hands the climb along with it,
or it measures a different problem.  The general one is the same rule this
repo already applies to bench cells -- a spread across two readings is not
evidence about the underlying distribution until something rules out the
measurement being broken, and here the CONTROL ARM is what would have caught
it immediately: a wrong-key climb re-timed against itself is still self-
consistent, but its ratio to the right-key arm is meaningless.
