ORDERING -J's MOVE SCAN BY IC INSTEAD OF BY THE TARGET MODEL
============================================================

  python3 eval/jorder_ab.py --tsv --trials 300 --length L --restarts R \
          --schedule S --seed {4242,99}

  -f -l wehrmacht -c, 10-pair board hidden, rotor key given, authentic
  HG Nord decrypts.  24 cells, 300 paired trials each (200 at R=128 f10).
  The arms differ ONLY by -J against -K.  JUDGED ON BREAK50 -- the count of
  trials recovering at least half the plaintext.

  (Measured while the IC order was $ENIGMA_JORDER=ic on top of -J, before it
  became the -K climb rule.  Verified byte-identical across the promotion --
  same answer and same plugboards-scored count on 6 fixtures of schedule x
  -R, and -K == -J --ic-order == the old env arm.)

VERDICT: cheaper everywhere, and BETTER on the schedules whose pre-pass does
not already supply IC.  Never measurably worse.


THE HEADLINE, BY SCHEDULE (lengths 60/80/100/120 at R=32)
----------------------------------------------------------

  schedule  cells  break50 target/ic   Stouffer Z   plugboards
  f10          8      629 / 766         -5.81       -25.1%
  m4f10        4      481 / 510         -2.32       -11.5%
  k4f10        4      498 / 496         +0.41       -11.0%
  i4f10        4      431 / 431         +0.10       -11.1%

(negative Z = IC ordering ahead; f10 is eight cells because it was run on two
seeds, the others on one.)

THE SPLIT HAS A MECHANISM, AND IT IS THE OBVIOUS ONE.  The schedules that gain
are exactly those whose pre-pass does NOT feed IC into the climb:

  f10     no pre-pass at all          -> large gain
  m4f10   monogram pre-pass           -> gain
  k4f10   mono+IC pre-pass            -> nothing
  i4f10   IC pre-pass                 -> nothing

Where the schedule already supplies IC information, ordering by IC adds none.
Where it does not, the order carries information the climb otherwise lacks.


THE f10 RESULT IS THE LARGE ONE AND IT REPLICATED
--------------------------------------------------

  L      seed 4242            seed 99
  60     27 / 33   (-1.00)    26 / 28   (-0.37)
  80     54 / 64   (-1.31)    55 / 73   (-2.50)
  100    96 / 114  (-2.01)   101 / 119  (-2.12)
  120   130 / 153  (-2.41)   140 / 182  (-4.70)

Eight of eight cells favour IC ordering, the effect grows monotonically with
length in both seeds, and the second seed is STRONGER than the first.  Pooled
Stouffer Z = -5.81.  The strongest cell is L=120 seed 99: 182 breaks of 300
against 140, i.e. 60.7% against 46.7% (+14.0pp), while the IC arm scores 25%
fewer plugboards.  Mean %-correct 52.3 -> 64.6 and exact 128 -> 165 in that
same cell, as secondary -- the judge here is BREAK50 throughout.

That is the unusual part: on f10 this is not a speed-for-quality trade in
either direction.  It is cheaper AND better, and the gap widens with message
length -- which is what the cost model predicts, the replaced scan being
linear in L where the histogram form is flat.


THAT LAST COLUMN IS PLUGBOARDS SCORED, NOT COMPUTE
---------------------------------------------------

  The IC ranking's O(26) work per move happens OUTSIDE the counted score loop,
  so score_iter prices the scans -K removes and not the work it adds.  It is
  the --polish gain-scan trap with the sign reversed.

  MEASURED SEPARATELY, on wall time -- eval/results-jorder-speed.txt, 24
  paired fixtures per cell against a self-control arm:

    schedule  L      -K / -J    95% CI          control   counter
    f10       60     -12.9%   [-13.7, -12.1]     -0.1%    -25.1%
    f10      100     -15.8%   [-16.8, -14.8]     -0.2%    -25.1%
    m4f10    100      -8.9%   [ -9.7,  -8.0]     +0.1%    -11.5%
    i4f10    100      -8.6%   [-10.0,  -7.1]     -0.3%    -11.1%
    k4f10    100      -8.2%   [ -9.6,  -6.8]     -0.1%    -11.0%
    k4f10     60      -4.1%   [ -5.0,  -3.2]     +0.8%    -11.0%

  -K IS FASTER EVERYWHERE MEASURED, and the saving GROWS WITH LENGTH: the work
  removed is linear in L (325 full decodes a restart) where the work added is
  nearly flat (325 O(26) deltas).  The counter's overstatement shrinks with
  length accordingly, 2.7x at L=60 down to 1.3x at L=100 on k4f10, so it is
  not a fixed multiple of the truth and cannot be corrected by a constant.

  eval/jorder_ab.py --match-compute calibrates that ratio per cell for anyone
  who wants to SPEND the saving on restarts rather than bank it.  That A/B has
  NOT been run: at high -R the reallocation is worth only ~0.3-0.9pp by the
  documented restart curve, and a 30-trial probe at -R 1024 read the arms dead
  level on break50 at L=60 (14/14 of 30) and L=100 (24/24 of 30).

THE PLUGBOARD SAVING SPLITS THE SAME WAY AS THE QUALITY, FOR THE SAME REASON
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------

  -25% on f10, -11% on the other three.

probe_toggle already takes the O(26) histogram path when hist_model() is true
-- IC, mono, monoic.  So a k4/i4/m4 pre-pass stage costs nothing to order
either way, and only the f10 TARGET stage's scans are replaced.  With no
pre-pass at all, every scan in the run is replaced, and the saving doubles.


THE ONE ADVERSE CELL DID NOT REPLICATE
---------------------------------------

  k4f10 L=100 R=128:  seed 4242  208 / 195  (z=+2.26, target ahead)
                      seed 99    208 / 210  (z=-0.45)

Pooled Z=+1.28, nothing.  The first reading looked like "IC ordering degrades
at high restart budgets", which would have been a real concern -- CLAUDE.md
records that pre-pass questions are restart-budget dependent.  It was a single
cell.  Across all 24 cells, four exceed |z|=2 in both directions, which is
about what 24 cells produce when some effects are real and the rest is
scatter.

The restart ladder on k4f10 (L=100) is flat throughout: R=8 z=+0.51, R=32
z=0.00, R=128 z=+2.26/-0.45.

f10's win DOES attenuate with budget -- L=120 reads z=-4.70 at R=32 and
z=-1.23 at R=128 (200 trials) -- which is expected: more restarts wash out a
difference in visit order.  It stays positive, and the compute saving is
unchanged at -24.9%.


WHAT IS STILL NOT MEASURED
---------------------------

  * PROSE.  Telegraphic German throughout.  CLAUDE.md records that scoring
    results do not transfer between the two writing styles, and this is a
    scoring-adjacent change.
  * -q AND -a TARGETS.  Both pay the same full-decode scan and should behave
    like f10, but were not run.
  * THE FULL SWEEP.  Rotor key given throughout, so this is the plugboard
    tier only -- which is where the move order lives, but not what an
    operator runs.
  * m4f10 ON A SECOND SEED.  Its Z=-2.32 rests on four cells from one seed.
    f10 was confirmed; this was not.


RECOMMENDATION
--------------

Default ON is defensible on this evidence: never measurably worse, ~11%
cheaper on the recommended telegraphic recipes, and both much cheaper and
substantially better on a bare fused target.  The case against is that prose
is unmeasured and the change is a search change, so it is left opt-in here
pending that.

The strongest practical reading is narrower and safe: IF YOU RUN A FUSED
TARGET WITH NO LOW-ORDER PRE-PASS, USE -K IN PLACE OF -J.  It is a quarter
cheaper and breaks materially more messages, and the advantage grows with
message length.
