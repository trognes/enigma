The probe microkernel on the Mac mini M1 -- metal/DESIGN.md 17.6 (ii)
=====================================================================

metal/probe_host.cc via $ENIGMA_GPU_PROBE=1 on metal/enigma-metal.  One
toggle probe -- apply a toggle, score the board -- repeated 64 times per
unit under one deterministic toggle sequence, in five arms doing
identical work; probes per second, the min of three ~0.4 s runs, every
unit's checksums verified against the tool's own score_components.
L=105 (the earlier fixture, 107 letters less two), -f, 64 lanes per
threadgroup.


Run 1 -- ONE start board, which is a confound; kept for what it says
--------------------------------------------------------------------

  arm                      units     probes/s   vs lane   cap  check
  lane (today)            139456     70967882     1.00x   512  ok
  group K=32 shuffle       56044     32852392     0.46x  1024  ok
  group K=32 packed        58118     32686511     0.46x   896  ok
  group K=16 packed       102368     49890343     0.70x   896  ok
  group K=8 packed        149840     63523033     0.90x   896  ok

THE INSTRUMENT IS VALID.  All five arms compiled at the first attempt
and every unit of every arm reproduced score_components bit for bit --
the packed board, the nine-word histogram, the boundary quads by
shuffle-up and the xor butterfly all agree with the CPU across 64
toggles from a ten-pair kicked board.  The register caps are per
pipeline: the lean shuffle arm reads the full 1 024, the packed arms 896
(five board words and nine histogram words held by name), and the lane
arm 512 -- against the climb kernel's 384, which says the climb's scan
machinery around the scorer is what costs the rest.

EVERY GROUP ARM IS SLOWER THAN THE LANE, monotone in K.  Fit
K*T(K) = c*L + K*(R*log2 K + F) to the three packed rows (per-character
work plus per-probe reductions and fixed cost, both scaling with K in
lane-time): per-character 0.89, R = 0.006, F = 0.009 in units of the
lane arm's probe time.  So the per-character step in the group shape
costs 0.89 of the lane's -- shuffles and packed-ALU lookups replaced the
thread-memory reads and the step barely moved -- and the reductions are
what separate K=8 from K=32.  Even with the reductions free the group
shape would reach ~1.1x the lane.

THE CONFOUND.  Every unit ran the SAME board under the same toggles, so
in the lane arm all 32 lanes of a simdgroup read the same rows[] entry
and gathered the same table cell at every character -- a broadcast and
one cache line, where a climb's 32 lanes hold 32 boards and touch 32.
The group arms gain nothing from it, their lanes reading different
positions by design; so the bias is one-way, against them.  The tell is
the lane arm's absolute rate: 71M probes/s against 22M for the
fixed-pass climb doing nominally the same scoring (results-gpu-
ablation-m1.txt run 4: 44.8 ns per probe at L=107), a 3.2x that the cap
(512 against 384) does not cover.  Either the broadcast is worth ~2x to
the lane arm, or the climb's scan machinery costs twice the scoring
itself.  Both matter and this run cannot tell them apart.

Fixed by giving unit u start board u % 64, the 64 boards being the
kicks for (key 0, restart 0..63), so a simdgroup holds 32 boards as a
climb does, with 64 oracles in place of one.  Run 2 is that.

THE BROADCAST WAS WORTH 6%.  The lane arm fell 71.0M -> 66.7M with 32
different boards per simdgroup, and the group arms did not move, as
predicted.  So the run-1 reading stands, sharpened: refitting the K
trend puts the group shape's per-character step at 0.94 of the lane's
in lane-time, with the reductions at ~0.009 per butterfly step per
lane and no measurable fixed cost.

17.4'S DECOMPOSITION IS DEAD.  Spreading the decode across a simdgroup
-- the board in registers by shuffle or by packed words, the histogram
in registers, no per-lane array anywhere -- does not shorten the
per-character step.  Converting both arms to lane-cycles per character
(rate x L over the resident lanes the cap allows, at ~1.28 GHz): the
lane arm's step is ~750 cycles and the K=8 packed arm's ~1 300.  Both
shapes are stalled for nearly all of a ~15-50-instruction step, and the
one with NO thread memory is the slower.  Whatever the ~750 cycles are,
they are not the thread-memory board and histogram that 17.2 ranked
first; table B had already priced those at 32% and 8%, and this is the
same fact from the other side.

THE FINDING IS THE OTHER NUMBER.  The lane arm -- mc_components on a
toggled board, which is exactly what the climb does 326 times a pass --
runs 66.7M probes/s.  The fixed-pass climb runs 22.3M (results-gpu-
ablation-m1.txt run 4, 44.8 ns per probe at L=107).  The same scoring,
3.0x slower inside the climb kernel than beside it, with the broadcast
now excluded.  The cap accounts for 512/384 = 1.33x of that at most;
the remaining ~2.2x is the kernel around the scorer: the toggle scan's
kind logic and cap check, the mutate and restore writes to a
thread-memory board (up to eight a probe against the probe arm's
two-to-four), the best-move compare, and whatever the compiler makes of
mc_components inlined inside a function large enough to be
register-starved.  17.2 ranked four suspects and none of them was this,
because every ablation removed part of the SCORER and the scan
machinery was in the "everything else" of table B (32.6%) and in the
per-probe intercept table D put at 3% -- which it cannot be, if the
probe arm is right.  One of those two instruments is misreading the
scan, and the probe arm is the one checked against the tool's scorer.

Two arms settle it, both cheap:
  lane packed    K = 1 through the group template: the board and the
                 histogram in registers in TODAY's decomposition, no
                 shuffles, no reductions -- whether the thread-memory
                 traffic matters at all once the scan is gone;
  climb pass     one steepest-ascent pass per unit, the real scan loop
                 from climb_body.h, 326 probes -- the scan machinery's
                 cost in the same harness as the scorer alone.

--------------------------------------------------------

  climb pass (scan)         1664     17656010     0.26x   384  ok

  group K=32 packed        55970     32591449     0.49x   832  ok
  group K=16 packed       105592     49177947     0.74x   832  ok
  group K=8 packed        149336     62238359     0.93x   832  ok

THE SCAN MACHINERY IS 74% OF THE CLIMB.  The real scan loop -- mc_pass,
16 passes of 326 scored boards, exactly one fixed-pass climb's work --
runs the scorer at 0.26x the rate the scorer alone runs it: 17.7M
probes/s against 66.7M, cap 384 against 512.  Occupancy covers 1.33x of
the 3.8x; the remaining 2.8x is what the scan loop does around each
score.  Against the ablation table's fixed-pass climb (22.3M) the pass
arm is 21% slower, which is the stage mix and not a disagreement: half
of table B's sixteen passes were the k4 stage, a 26-bin histogram
model that is far cheaper per probe than the fused target the pass arm
runs for all sixteen.

AND TABLE D WAS NOT WRONG EITHER.  Its 3% intercept says the scan adds
no FIXED cost per probe, and this run says the scan costs 2.8x per
probe -- both true, if the scan's cost is per-CHARACTER: a multiplier
on the slope, which a linear fit folds into b and cannot see.  What
does that is the toggle's kind branch.  `if (paired) { remove; score;
restore } else { force; score; restore }` is one call to mc_key on the
CPU and TWO on a GPU whenever any lane of the simdgroup is in each
branch: with 32 boards of ~10 pairs each, a-b is paired somewhere in
the simdgroup on most of the 325 toggles, and the whole scorer then
runs twice with lanes masked.  Not thread-memory latency, not the
gather: the scorer executed twice.  Fixed by a single branch-free
mutate/score/restore for all four kinds (climb_body.h, mc_pass): same
writes, same scores, same order, same tie rule -- verify_identity.py
says byte-identical -- and one mc_key per toggle.  The pass arm is the
measurement of it: run 4.

THE REGISTER-RESIDENT LANE IS 1.52x, ALL OF IT OCCUPANCY.  Today's
decomposition with the board packed into five words and the histogram
into nine, no thread memory at all, runs 101.2M probes/s at cap 896
against 66.7M at cap 512.  896/512 = 1.75x more lanes resident for
1.52x more throughput, so the per-lane step is ~15% SLOWER (the select
chains cost more ALU than the thread-memory reads cost latency, which
is the group arms' finding again) and the win is entirely that the
kernel holds more of them.  It is the best per-probe rate any shape
here has reached, and it beats the best group arm (K=8 packed, 62.2M)
by 1.63x: 17.4 is dead against the fair comparison as well as the
unfair one.

THE PASS ARM WENT 17.7M -> 33.0M, 1.87x, from the branch-free toggle
alone, and every other row is where it was.  The unit's checksum is
the same integer as before and verify_identity.py is unchanged.

THE CLIMB DID NOT MOVE, AND THAT CORRECTS THE READING ABOVE.  The real
climb at this cell, -R 256 over 26 keys, 64 lanes, read 2 401 climbs/s
before the fix, 2 402 after it, and 2 432 after a clean rebuild from
the merged tree -- the same fixture throughout.  Same code
change, +87% in one kernel and +0% in the other: the double scoring
was a property of how the probe's small pass kernel was COMPILED --
mc_key inlined once into each branch and never merged -- and not of
the scan loop.  In the climb kernel, large enough that mc_key was not
inlined twice, the compiler had already merged the two identical calls
into one.  So the 1.87x was the pass arm becoming honest, not the
climb becoming faster; the claim that "the climb carries it" was true
of the code and false of the speedup; and the 21% by which the old
pass arm trailed the fixed-pass climb, explained above as the stage
mix, was mostly this.  The branch-free form stays -- it is what makes
the pass arm a fair instrument, it is byte-identical, and it cannot
hurt -- but it is not a lever.

The corrected ledger, in probes per second at this cell:

  scorer alone, board in registers (lane packed regs)   101.5M   cap 896
  scorer alone, board in thread memory (lane)            66.5M   cap 512
  the scan loop around it (climb pass, honest)           33.0M   cap 384
  the climb at fixed passes, fused-equivalent            ~25M    cap 384
  the climb as it runs, divergence and try_repair in     12.2M

The scan loop costs 2.0x over the scorer it wraps; the climb's stage
loop, try_repair scans and cap-check divergence a further ~1.3x; and a
natural climb's own divergence (1.46x) and try_repair (1.33x) the rest.
Parity with the CPU (13.1k climbs/s, ~67M probes/s natural-equivalent)
would need the natural climb to run at the scorer-alone-in-registers
rate with every layer above it free, which is not on offer.  The one
layer still unmeasured is the scan loop on the packed board: run 5.
If it reaches the scorer's ~60M, a climb rebuilt on it projects to
roughly 60M x 0.76 / 1.46 / 1.33 = ~23M natural, ~4 500 climbs/s --
1.9x today's, a third of the CPU's.  That projection uses measured
factors for every layer and it is the honest ceiling of the current
design on this chip.

What is left between the scan and the scorer alone is 2.0x: the cap,
384 against 512, for 1.33x, and ~1.5x for the eight mutate-and-restore
writes per toggle to a thread-memory board, each ordered before the
scorer's reads, plus the compare.  The packed board addresses both --
the writes become select chains and the board five registers -- and
the lane arms have already shown what that buys the scorer (1.53x, all
occupancy).  Measured next as `climb pass packed`: mc_pass() on mc_pk
line for line, its checksum against the ARRAY pass's oracle so the two
representations are proven to agree before anything is built into the
climb.

THE PACKED BOARD IS WORTH 1.48x IN THE SCAN, AND THE CAP DID NOT MOVE.
mc_pass_pk runs 48.7M probes/s against mc_pass's 33.0M, every unit
reproducing the array pass's integers on the GPU as on the CPU.  The
cap stays at 384 -- so unlike the scorer-alone case (512 -> 896) the
gain here is not occupancy but the per-lane step: the eight
thread-memory writes per toggle are gone.  What pins the scan kernel's
registers is the scan's own live state -- best and move scores in 64
bits, the move's kind and endpoints, the loop counters, sa/sb/x/y/
xx/yy/na/nb -- not the board, which is why the board moving to
registers did not free any.

The scan's overhead over its scorer is therefore unchanged in ratio:
48.7M against the register-resident scorer's 101.2M is 2.08x, where
33.0M against 66.1M was 2.00x.  Moving the board made both faster by
the same factor and the machinery around the scorer still costs as
much as the scorer.

THE LEDGER IS COMPLETE.  Every layer of the current design is now
measured on a kernel that exists and checks against the tool:

  scorer alone, board in registers      101.2M
  scan loop on the packed board          48.7M   x 2.08 for the scan
  climb at fixed passes, projected       ~37M    x 1.3 stages, cap check
  climb as it runs, projected            ~19M    x 1.46 divergence,
                                                 x 1.33 try_repair

~19M natural probes/s at ~5 100 per climb is ~3 700 climbs/s: 1.5x
today's 2 430, and 0.28x the CPU's 13.1k at this cell.  The 17.4
decomposition is 0.49-0.94x of the lane and is dead.  There is no
unmeasured lever left in the lane-per-climb design: the board is in
registers, the scorer is one call per toggle, the double scoring was
never the climb's, and the remaining 8x between the scorer in
registers and the climb as it runs is the scan (2.1x), the stage
structure (1.3x), and the divergence and try_repair that a climb per
lane brings by construction (1.9x).

AND THE CPU COMPARISON IS GENEROUS TO THE GPU.  Every GPU/CPU ratio in
these files runs the CPU under steepest ascent, the rule the port
fixed as its contract; the tool's recommended recipe is -K, which does
~2.8x fewer scorings per climb.  The GPU could adopt the same rule and
the ratio would likely hold, but against the tool as actually used the
gap is wider than the tables say.

WHAT THIS IS AND IS NOT A VERDICT ON.  On Apple silicon the GPU cannot
reach its own CPU with this design: the M1 pairs 8 GPU cores of ~1 000
lanes each with 8 CPU cores, and every layer above says why.  The
pathology is per-lane and the M2 Pro cross-check said chip-independent
-- but the ABSOLUTE rate scales with resident lanes times clock, and a
discrete CUDA part carries ~30x the M1's lanes at ~2x the clock against
a desktop CPU of perhaps 2x the M1's.  The same kernel, unchanged,
projects to parity or a small multiple there -- arithmetic, with
section 12's record as the warning -- and enigma-cuda's 20k
key-climbs/s on a 2016 GTX 1070 says the order of magnitude is right.
The port's value, if it has one, is on that hardware and not on the
Mac; the Mac was the instrument, and the instrument's job is done.


WHERE THIS LEAVES THE PORT.  The scorer with a register-resident board
runs 101M probes/s; the climb ran 17.7M and now 33.0M.  That 5.7x is the scan
machinery plus the occupancy it costs, and both are inside the current
lane-per-climb design.  Parity with the CPU at this cell needs ~4.6x
(13.1k climbs/s against 2.83k).  The ceiling is not a redesign's
estimate this time; it is a measured rate of a kernel that exists,
scoring the same boards to the same integers.  The levers, in order:
one mc_key per toggle (above, the pass arm measures it next); the
packed board in the climb itself, so the mutate and restore are ALU
and the kernel's register footprint drops toward the 896 the lane arm
reads; and only then whatever the pass arm still shows above the
scorer alone.
