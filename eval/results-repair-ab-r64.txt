THE READING
===========

THE VALUE QUESTION IS ANSWERED: THE RE-PAIR IS WORTH MORE THAN THE
RESTARTS IT COSTS.  At -R 8 a 3-7% saving could not buy an integer
restart, so the matched-time arm ran the identical command to the
matched-restart arm at two of four lengths and the question could not be
put.  At -R 64 it can: `off+` gets 76 to 84 restarts against `on`'s 64,
a real 19-31% bonus.  It never wins.  `on` takes L=80 outright
(78 on-only discordants against 50, p = 0.017) and ties at L=60
(52/51, p = 1.000) and L=100 (76/68, p = 0.560); at L=40 `off+` leads
59 to 54 and does not resolve (p = 0.383) in a cell where 2.7% of
trials break at all.

AND THE CONCLUSION IS ROBUST TO THE COST RATIO BEING WRONG, WHICH
MATTERS BECAUSE IT PROBABLY IS.  See below: two pilots on the identical
fixtures disagree, 1.03-1.07 against 1.19-1.31.  The arm was built on
the HIGHER pair, so `off+` was handed more restarts than a 1.05 ratio
would justify -- roughly 67 rather than 76-84.  Fewer restarts can only
make `off+` weaker, so every cell above is conservative for `on` and the
verdict cannot flip in the direction the uncertainty runs.

THE RE-PAIR'S VALUE GROWS WITH RESTARTS RATHER THAN FADING.  That is
the opposite of what --polish does, and it is the interesting part.
CLAUDE.md records the finisher's edge halving from R80 to R160 and
sometimes vanishing by 160, because restarts subsume the near-solution
boards it targets.  The re-pair does not behave that way -- matched
restarts, on minus off:

    L      -R 8        -R 64
    40     4 (ns)      3 (ns)
    60     4 (ns)     21 (p = 0.028)
    80    42          58
   100    54          62

L=60 goes from nothing to significant as the budget rises eightfold.
The mechanism is structural: --polish fires ONCE on the best board after
every restart, so more restarts dilute its share, while try_repair fires
at EVERY convergence inside EVERY restart, so its contribution scales
with the restarts rather than against them.  A finisher and a
barrier-cross are not the same kind of thing and do not answer to the
same budget argument.

WHAT THE MATCHED-TIME MARGIN SAYS ABOUT SIZE.  At matched restarts the
re-pair is decisive (92/30 discordants at L=100); at matched time it is
level (76/68).  So its benefit is worth approximately what its cost
would buy in extra restarts, and slightly more at L=80.  It is not a
large lever in either direction -- it is a cheap one that pays for
itself, which is exactly the case for leaving a default alone.


THE COST RATIO IS UNRESOLVED, AND BOTH PILOTS CANNOT BE RIGHT
=============================================================

Same 24 fixtures, same seed, same slope between -R 8 and -R 128:

    L      run 1 (min of 2)   run 2 (min of 5)
    40     1.033              1.309
    60     1.337 (rejected)   1.186
    80     1.046              1.196
   100     1.072              1.262

Run 2 is the better-FORMED of the two by the criterion that condemned
run 1's L=60 cell: both its columns rise monotonically with L (on
0.272/0.302/0.338/0.392, off 0.208/0.255/0.283/0.311), where run 1's
`on` column did not.  But every absolute time in run 2 is 20-50% higher,
and min-of-5 cannot exceed min-of-2 on the same work -- so the box was
slower, not the statistic noisier.  The container had restarted between
the runs (uptime read 6 minutes at 21:26 and 5 minutes at 22:13), so the
two were measured on different machine states.

NEITHER NUMBER SHOULD BE QUOTED until one is reproduced on a quiet box.
What is safe to say is the range, 1.03-1.31, and that the re-pair is
cheap at every point in it.  The recovery arms are untouched by this:
they are paired, deterministic under a fixed seed, and machine speed
cannot change which trials break.  Only R' depends on the ratio, and the
direction of that dependence is what makes the verdict above safe.

The pilot measures `on` first in both arms' loop.  Any warm-up or
decaying disturbance therefore inflates `on` and biases the ratio UP,
which is one more reason the true value likely sits nearer run 1's end
of the range than run 2's.  Alternating the arm order per rep would
remove it and is the obvious fix if this is ever re-measured.
