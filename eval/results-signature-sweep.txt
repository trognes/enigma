== --signature-sweep: real per-key cost ==

Measured with the mode IN THE TOOL (eval/ had only extrapolations from hypothesis
counts, which were 2.4x too pessimistic).  17576 keys (-g ...), n=111, -T 4,
recipe -c -f -l wehrmacht -S i4f10 -J --polish -u B -w 231 -r AAA.

arm                          wall      score_iter    per key    hypotheses
-R 1                         3.66 s     40315293      208 us    --
-R 4                        13.29 s    161607341      756 us    --
-R 16                       70.43 s    645358805     4007 us    --
--signature-seed 1           1.14 s      8026136       65 us     20
--signature-seed 5           4.77 s     44920809      271 us     20
--sweep L>=9 K=5            12.01 s     39841350      683 us    ~850
--sweep L>=7 K=1            16.81 s      5212229      956 us    1883
--sweep L>=7 K=5            19.98 s     30254355     1137 us    1883

Sweeping is 3.5x CHEAPER than -R 16, not dearer: 94x the hypotheses costs only
14.7x the wall time, because most hypotheses die on the first contradiction.

score_iter is the WRONG AXIS here and says the opposite: --sweep L>=7 K=1 scores
FEWER plugboards than terminal K=1 (5.2M vs 8.0M) while taking 15x the wall time,
because its seeds are more constrained so the climbs are cheap and the uncounted
deduction dominates.

== ranking, at the TRUE key (eval/ probe) ==

                        L>=7        L>=9      terminal
messages covered        16/66       11/66     7/66
correct seed exists     45/48       27/33     200/200
IC top-1                25/45 56%   15/27 56% 150/200 75%
IC top-5                30/45 67%   19/27 70% ~84% (top-3)

Raising the floor to 9 halves the cost and does NOT improve the ranking, so the
residual failures are IC failing to separate, not the haystack being large.

Reliability tracks the SIGNATURE LENGTH sharply: on one constructed fixture a
6-letter mid-message doubling was not recovered even at K=100, while 9 and 13
letters recovered at K=1.

== K sweep: cost AND gain, on a real 676-key sweep ==

32 trials over the 16 corpus messages carrying a 7+ doubling anywhere, -g A..,
10-pair board hidden, -T 4.

arm         mean %   exact     wall/trial  per key   score_iter
-R 1        17.9     4/32      0.23 s       334 us    1544746
-R 4        28.8     7/32      0.61 s       897 us    6158178
-R 16       48.9     13/32     2.26 s      3345 us   24635219
terminal K5 51.1     13/32     0.25 s       368 us    1428322
swept K1    60.6     16/32     0.89 s      1319 us     209393
swept K5    75.3     21/32     0.99 s      1461 us    1164490
swept K10   81.1     23/32     1.13 s      1675 us    2519731
swept K20   81.2     23/32     1.44 s      2136 us    5505752

K=10 IS THE OPERATING POINT.  K=20 buys nothing -- same 23/32, same mean -- for
28% more time.  This refutes the prediction that sweeping wants a high K, which
came from the probe's top-20 recall (35/45 against top-5's 30/45) measured AT THE
TRUE KEY.  In a sweep, raising K helps the true key AND all 675 wrong keys, each
of which also gets K seeds and a better best-of-K score; the extra recall is real
but does not convert into discrimination.

Marginal cost per climb RISES with K -- 35.5, 42.8, 46.1 us across K=1->5->10->20
-- because lower-ranked seeds are less constrained, so their climbs are dearer.
Estimates made before measuring were 1360 us (K=10) and 1810 us (K=20), i.e. low
by 23% and 15%, in the direction that caveat predicted.

Against the baseline: swept K=10 breaks 23/32 against -R 16's 13/32 in HALF the
wall time.  Even K=1 beats -R 16 by 3 breaks at 40% of the cost.

score_iter misleads again: swept K1 has the LOWEST count of any arm (0.21M, 7x
below -R 1) while taking 4x -R 1's wall time.
