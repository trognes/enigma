THE OPERATIONAL NULL -- climbed decrypts at WRONG rotor keys
   5888 texts, mean length 116

1. CLOSED FORM from this population's letter statistics
   X-rate 2.41%   A 0.05081   ->  4.93e-06   (1 in 202,699)

   The X-rate is why this population is SAFER than the shuffled
   real decrypts the entry used to quote (X 6.84%%): the rule needs
   an X separator and the rate is ~linear in how often one occurs.
   A climb maximises an n-gram score and German prose is X-poor, so
   climbing a WRONG key drives X down -- 2.41% here against 5.58%
   in true-key decrypts.

2. DIRECT COUNT over the stored climbed decrypts
   0 hits in 5888, against 0.03 expected -- so 0 is the likely
   outcome and bounds nothing.  Neither did the recorded '0 false
   positives in 8928', which expects 0.04.

3. TRIALS on synthetic text FITTED TO THIS POPULATION
   The closed form assumes memoryless letters.  On real German that
   fails: a bigram chain fitted to the CORPUS fires ~9x more often
   than its letter frequencies predict.  Does that carry over?

   bigram IC / independence prediction  (1.00 = memoryless)
     climbed wrong-key text   1.07
     real (true-key) decrypts 1.48   <- where the 9x came from

   generator                          hits        rate vs closed form
   i.i.d. from climbed freqs             6    4.00e-06  0.8x
   bigram Markov from climbed           10    6.67e-06  1.4x
   pooled                               16    5.33e-06  95% CI [3.0e-06, 8.7e-06]

   The closed form sits inside that interval, and the Markov/i.i.d.
   gap is not significant -- climbed wrong-key text is nearly
   memoryless, so the corpus's 9x structure penalty does not apply.
   TAKE 5e-06, i.e. 1 in 202,699.

4. WHAT THAT ALLOWS -- expected false hits = candidates x rate

   climbed keys       expected false hits
   10,000             0.05

   Against that, ONE true key -- and it carries a qualifying
