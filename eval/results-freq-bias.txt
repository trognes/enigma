WHAT THE LOW-ORDER PRE-PASS CAN SEE: ciphertext frequency and the plugboard
===========================================================================

  eval/proto_freqbias.cc      the 325-plug IC ranking, by letter frequency
  eval/seed_letter_bias.py    which letters a -S k4 seed actually plugs

Authentic HG Nord decrypts, rotor key given, 10-pair board hidden,
wehrmacht tables, ENIGMA_SEED=0.


THE STRUCTURE
-------------

IC reads only the histogram of the decrypt taken BEFORE the exit board, and
cooc_col(c, d) accumulates only over positions where ct_i == c -- so that
column's total mass IS count(c).  Plugging (a,b) on an empty board perturbs
the histogram by -ma -mb +qa +qb, of mass exactly count(a) + count(b) =: m.

Two consequences, and only the second survives measurement.

The EXIT board is invisible to IC (steck is an involution, so the post-board
histogram is the pre-board one permuted, and IC is permutation-invariant).
That is already exploited -- it is the basis of the histogram-form scoring in
src/scoring.cc.

A systematic upward drift with m does NOT exist.  Writing n = n0 + delta, the
term ||delta||^2 is strictly positive and proportional to m, which looks like
a first-order bias -- but delta is not independent of n0.  ma is literally a
component of n0 (n0 = sum_c cooc_col(c,c)), so -2<n0,ma> cancels the +||ma||^2
exactly in expectation.  Measured corr(IC score, m) = -0.003 / +0.012 /
-0.017 / -0.011 / -0.025 at L = 100 / 40 / 60 / 167 / 300.  There is nothing
there.


INFLUENCE, NOT IMPROVEMENT
--------------------------

325 plugs in quintiles by m, scores centred per key, x1000, L = 100, 800 keys:

  quintile   mean m   wrong mu   wrong sd   true mu
         1      4.2      +0.28      11.68     +5.43
         3      7.5      -0.36      15.01     +9.13
         5     11.5      -0.83      18.14    +13.58

A WRONG plug on frequent letters does not improve the IC -- its mean effect is
flat and slightly negative (-0.046 sd at the top quintile), monotone and
consistently signed at L = 60/100/167.  What grows is the SPREAD, as sqrt(m):
1.55x over a 2.74x range in m, against sqrt(2.74) = 1.66, which is the
random-sign sum over ~m bins.  The TRUE-plug mean grows as m itself, 2.5x over
the same range.

So SNR grows as sqrt(m).  A correct plug on frequent letters is genuinely more
detectable, by about the square root of the frequency ratio.


THE RANKING IS THEREFORE ALREADY CORRECT -- do not "de-bias" it
--------------------------------------------------------------

True-plug mean rank of 325 under the raw IC ranking, split at each key's
median count sum (chance = 163.0):

    L      all   frequent    rare    gap as % of the enrichment
   40    141.8      136.2   150.6    68%
   60    132.7      127.3   140.7    44%
  100    111.7      103.0   122.4    38%
  167     89.2       79.4   101.0    29%
  300     55.6       49.3    63.3    13%

The heterogeneity is largest exactly where signal is scarcest: at forty
letters nearly the whole of IC's single-plug enrichment is carried by the
frequent half.

But correcting for it buys NOTHING.  Residualising the score on m, or
studentising by sqrt(m), moves the mean rank by under a point at every length
(L = 100: raw 111.7, resid 111.3, stud 111.9).  The reason is that the signal
scales with m as well as the noise, so the frequency weighting is not a
miscalibration -- it is the correct weighting.  Studentising stops rare-letter
true plugs competing against high-variance frequent-letter decoys, but strips
frequent-letter true plugs of an advantage they earned; the two cancel, and it
redistributes rank between the halves without changing the total.


DOES THE PRE-PASS ACT ON THIS?  YES, AT ABOUT THE PREDICTED STRENGTH
--------------------------------------------------------------------

-S k4, L = 100, plug-weighted mean ciphertext count over unweighted:

                              -R 300 (kicked)   -R 0 (IC alone)
  seed plugs per board                   7.65              4.00
  plug-weighted / unweighted            1.034             1.106
  true plug found, frequent letters     97.7%             10.0%
  true plug found, rare letters         87.4%              6.5%

THE KICKED COLUMN MEASURES THE KICK, NOT IC, and this is the easy mistake:
with the default 10-pair kick a k4 cap only blocks ADDS, so the seed keeps
7.65 plugs, most of them the kick's uniform draws.  Any arithmetic treating
the stage-0 seed as a 4-plug board is about the wrong object.  At -R 0 the cap
binds exactly (4.00) and every plug is IC's own choice.

The 1.106 enrichment sits at the sqrt rate the SNR argument predicts.  For
c ~ Binomial(100, 1/26), selection proportional to sqrt(count) gives 1.140 and
proportional to count gives 1.250; no bias gives 1.000.  And a true plug on
frequent letters is found 1.54x more often (10.0% against 6.5%).


WHAT DOES NOT FOLLOW: restricting the pre-pass to the frequent letters
----------------------------------------------------------------------

MEASURED END TO END AND IT LOSES (eval/noplug_prepass_ab.py).  600 paired
trials, L = 100, -R 8, --random 4, -J, -S i4 then f10, rotor key given,
10-pair board hidden, winner chosen by SCORE.  Arm B pins the rare 13 letters
empty with --no-plug through stage 0 only; the f10 continuation has every
letter free in both arms, and the frequent half is chosen from the ciphertext,
so arm B is a realizable attack rather than an oracle.

  arm                 restarts   >=50% of letters   mean %correct   seeds
  A  all 26 letters          8         114/600             26.4      6.87
  B  frequent 13 only        8          87/600             22.4      4.62

Discordant 67 only-A against 40 only-B, McNemar z = -2.61 (p = 0.009).  That
is -4.5pp absolute, 95% CI [1.1, 7.9] against B, a 24% RELATIVE loss of
breaks, and the lower-variance mean agrees at -4.0pp.

AND THE SAVING CANNOT BUY IT BACK.  Fitted as a slope over -R 0/128/512/1024
(never a floor subtraction -- at -R 8 an invocation is ~95% process startup):

  stage 0 alone     A  67.0 us/restart    B  36.5    B is 1.84x cheaper
  full i4f10        A 443.2 us/restart

so the f10 continuation is ~376 us and identical in both arms, putting B at
412.7 against 443.2 overall -- only 7.4% cheaper, i.e. -R 8.6 at A's cost.
Interpolating the documented climb curve (-R 2/4/8/16 -> 50/68/79/87% at
L = 167) a 1.074x restart increase is worth about +0.8pp against the 4.5pp
lost.  Breaking even would need a ~1.6x whole-run saving, which is more than
the entire pre-pass costs at this kick.

NOTE THE KICK SIZE SETS THE LEVER.  At --random 4 the pre-pass is only 15% of
the staged climb, well under the 39-54% documented for the default 10-pair
kick: from a 4-pair kick the cap-4 stage starts already at the cap and barely
moves.  A larger kick would give the restriction more to save -- and also more
seed diversity to destroy.

THE MECHANISM IS THE SEED COLUMN: 4.62 distinct seeds of 8 against 6.87, a 33%
collapse.  That is the trade predicted before the run -- ~4x fewer reachable
true plugs against ~1.5x better per-plug accuracy -- and it lands the same way
the 10-plug-versus-4-plug kick result did (ENHANCEMENTS.md: the bigger kick
breaks 286 of 500 against 219 while having a LOWER per-trial success rate).
Diversity beats per-draw accuracy here, twice now.

AND MOST OF THE LOSS IS THAT COLLAPSE, NOT THE RESTRICTION
-----------------------------------------------------------

The run above compares the arms at equal RESTARTS, which confounds
"restricting the pre-pass" with "exploring less".  --seed-ladder separates
them: arm A at -R 8 reaches 6.70 distinct seeds and arm B needs -R 17 to reach
6.67, i.e. 2.13x the restarts.  Re-run at that pair, same 600 trials:

  comparison                       arm A      arm B    effect        z
  equal restarts     8 vs 8      114/600     87/600    -4.5pp    -2.61
  matched diversity  8 vs 17     114/600    105/600    -1.5pp    -0.87

The residual is -1.5pp with a 95% CI of [-4.9, +1.9], and the mean %-correct
agrees at -1.5pp (26.4 against 24.9).  So roughly 3.0 of the original 4.5pp
was exploration and the rest does not resolve.  Arm B was in fact given
slightly MORE diversity than A (7.05 against 6.87 realised) and still came out
behind on the point estimate.

The practical verdict does not soften, it changes shape: the restriction needs
2.13x the restarts to draw LEVEL, and level is the best reading available.  It
buys nothing; it merely stops actively costing once you pay to undo its own
side effect.  Settling whether the -1.5pp residual is real would take ~2400
trials, which does not change that.

THE COSTS ARE EVEN ONLY BECAUSE DUPLICATE SEEDS ARE CLIMBED ONCE, and that
ties this to --seed-dedup.  Stage 0 is 8 x 67.0 = 536 us for A against
17 x 36.5 = 620 us for B, and the continuations are 6.70 against 6.67 climbs
at ~376 us, so 3055 us against 3128 us -- within 2.4%.  In the shipped tool
arm B would run 17 f10 climbs against 8 and cost about double, unless
duplicate seeds are skipped.  Arm B's duplicate rate at -R 17 is 61%, well
above the 43% an unrestricted -R 1000 reaches, so a restricted pre-pass is
exactly the regime where --seed-dedup pays: the restriction is affordable only
because the flag exists, and even then it does not win.

The finding's real content is the opposite of an opportunity: the pre-pass is
ALREADY selecting frequent letters, at about the strength the theory predicts,
so there is no headroom in steering it further.


ONE SCOPE LIMIT: THIS IS AN IC PROPERTY AND DOES NOT TRANSFER TO -f
-------------------------------------------------------------------

The frequency weighting exists because the exit board is invisible to IC.  For
the n-gram targets it is fully visible -- p_i = steck[q_i] -- so a plug (a,b)
additionally affects every position where the pre-exit decrypt lands on a or b,
which is n_a + n_b ~ 2L/26 positions regardless of how rare a and b are in the
CIPHERTEXT.  At L = 100 that is a roughly constant floor of ~7.7 positions on
top of the entry side's 4.2 (quintile 1) to 11.5 (quintile 5), so the influence
ratio falls from 2.74x for IC to about 1.6x for the target model.

Rare-letter plugs are therefore much less neglected by -f than by the pre-pass,
which is the division of labour the staged schedule already has.  A break needs
all ten pairs; rare ones are only cheap to ignore in the stage that could not
see them anyway.
