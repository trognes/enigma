Common-letter first-plug exhaust vs matched -R restarts
=======================================================

Question: does EXHAUSTIVELY seeding the plugboard on the high-frequency
letters -- the Sullivan/Weierud instinct -- beat spending the same compute on
plain -R restarts?  Measured, not argued.

Why it is plausible.  Plug pairs came off the key sheet uncorrelated with
letter frequency (E is plugged as often as Q), but a wrong stecker on E
scrambles ~17% of the decrypt where a wrong stecker on X barely moves the
score.  So the score gradient, and the climb's local optima, concentrate on
the common letters, and seeding the search there is well motivated.  The
repo already banks this as a SOFT bias (--biased-random, +9.3% breaks at
low -R).  What was untested is the HARD form: exhausting the first plug over
the common letters specifically, cheaper than the general --exhaust 1 the
archive measured as dominated.

Harness: eval/common_exhaust_ab.py.  All arms paired on identical trials --
rotor key given, 10-pair board hidden, -c -K -S k4f10 -f -l wehrmacht, NO
--polish so the finisher cannot confound a question about the seed.

  E-common  first plug exhausted over every pair touching the top-6 letters
            of the WEHRMACHT monogram table -- ENXRSI, 135 pairs -- one
            pinned -R 0 climb per pair via -s, best score wins.  The letters
            come from the plaintext model the search scores against, not
            prose German: the sensitivity argument is about what is frequent
            in the text being scored, which is why the X separator is in.
  E-all     native --exhaust 1, all 325 pairs: the sanity arm.
  C-common  plain -R matched to E-common on plugboards scored (two-pass
  C-all     rescale; within 1% in every cell).

Judged on break50 (>=50% recovered), McNemar on the discordant pairs.  Wall
time is deliberately NOT the axis: a pinned single climb is ~90% process
startup, so the E arms look grossly expensive in seconds for a reason that
has nothing to do with the algorithm.  60 paired trials per length,
authentic HG-Nord decrypts.

    python3 eval/common_exhaust_ab.py --trials 60 --lengths 107 167 --topk 6

Findings
--------

1. IT DOES NOT PAY.  At L=107 the common-letter exhaust is a wash (+3
   breaks, 6 vs 3 discordant, p = 0.51 -- nothing).  At L=167 it LOSES:
   52/60 against 57/60, with ZERO discordant pairs in its favour against
   five for the control (p = 0.062), and mean %-correct down 6.9pp (88.5 vs
   95.4).  Pooled over both lengths the discordants are 6 vs 8 -- net
   nothing -- but the split by length is the finding.

2. THE SPLIT IS THE MECHANISM.  A pinned first plug helps only on the ~4 of
   135 hypotheses that are a true plug; every other climb starts handicapped
   by a WRONG forced plug that the pin keeps in place to convergence.  Where
   the climb is still failing (L=107, plenty of headroom) the ~4 correctly
   seeded climbs roughly buy back what the ~131 handicapped ones cost, and
   it washes.  Where a kicked restart already lands the truth reliably
   (L=167, C-common at 95%) handicapping 131 of 135 climbs is pure waste,
   and it loses.  The exhaust is worth least exactly where a message is
   most breakable.

3. THE COVERAGE WAS NOT THE PROBLEM.  A true plug sat among the 135 common
   pairs in 60/60 trials at both lengths, so the exhaust always HAD a
   correct hypothesis to climb from.  Having it is not enough; the best-of-
   135 has to pick it, and 131 handicapped competitors plus the decoy
   attraction the archive documents (the joint optimum of the strongest
   plugs sits above the truth) are what it is picked against.

4. THE SANITY ARM REPRODUCES "NO ADVANTAGE", NOT "DOMINATED".  Native
   --exhaust 1 ties or leans down against matched -R at both lengths (45 vs
   46 at 107, 57 vs 59 at 167; p = 1.0 / 0.5).  That is consistent with the
   archive's verdict that exhaust has no edge, but weaker than its 10-40pp
   figure (archived/PERFORMANCE.md 3.6).  The likely reason is regime: 3.6
   was short English prose under an older recipe; here a single first-plug
   exhaust under k4f10 / -K on telegraphic German at operational length is
   325 seeded climbs against 325 kicked ones, and they come out about level.
   Recorded rather than papered over.

5. MORE COMPUTE STILL BUYS MORE BREAKS, AND THAT IS ALL THE "ALL" PAIR
   SHOWS.  The 2.4x-compute pair breaks more than the common pair at both
   lengths (45-46 vs 40-43; 57-59 vs 52-57).  That is restarts converting
   into breaks -- the repo's standing result -- not an exhaust effect.

Verdict
-------

Sound insight, wrong verb.  The common letters DO carry the score
sensitivity, and exploiting that as a soft bias on the restart kick
(--biased-random) is measured to win.  Committing to it as an EXHAUSTION is
null where a message is hard and a measured loss where it is breakable, at
matched compute, because a pinned wrong plug handicaps a climb that a plain
restart would have run unencumbered.  The Sullivan/Weierud approach was the
right pragmatic move for its compute budget; with cheap parallel restarts
the same insight belongs in the kick, not in an exhaustive seed.  Nothing
here changes the search playbook: spend the compute on -R.
