== --signature-sweep: real per-key cost ==

Measured with the mode IN THE TOOL (eval/ had only extrapolations from hypothesis
counts, which were 2.4x too pessimistic).  17576 keys (-g ...), n=111, -T 4,
recipe -c -f -l wehrmacht -S i4f10 -J --polish -u B -w 231 -r AAA.

arm                          wall      score_iter    per key    hypotheses
-R 1                         3.66 s     40315293      208 us    --
-R 4                        13.29 s    161607341      756 us    --
-R 16                       70.43 s    645358805     4007 us    --
--signature-seed 1           1.14 s      8026136       65 us     20
--signature-seed 5           4.77 s     44920809      271 us     20
--sweep L>=9 K=5            12.01 s     39841350      683 us    ~850
--sweep L>=7 K=1            16.81 s      5212229      956 us    1883
--sweep L>=7 K=5            19.98 s     30254355     1137 us    1883

Sweeping is 3.5x CHEAPER than -R 16, not dearer: 94x the hypotheses costs only
14.7x the wall time, because most hypotheses die on the first contradiction.

score_iter is the WRONG AXIS here and says the opposite: --sweep L>=7 K=1 scores
FEWER plugboards than terminal K=1 (5.2M vs 8.0M) while taking 15x the wall time,
because its seeds are more constrained so the climbs are cheap and the uncounted
deduction dominates.

== ranking, at the TRUE key (eval/ probe) ==

                        L>=7        L>=9      terminal
messages covered        16/66       11/66     7/66
correct seed exists     45/48       27/33     200/200
IC top-1                25/45 56%   15/27 56% 150/200 75%
IC top-5                30/45 67%   19/27 70% ~84% (top-3)

Raising the floor to 9 halves the cost and does NOT improve the ranking, so the
residual failures are IC failing to separate, not the haystack being large.

Reliability tracks the SIGNATURE LENGTH sharply: on one constructed fixture a
6-letter mid-message doubling was not recovered even at K=100, while 9 and 13
letters recovered at K=1.

== K sweep: cost AND gain, on a real 676-key sweep ==

32 trials over the 16 corpus messages carrying a 7+ doubling anywhere, -g A..,
10-pair board hidden, -T 4.

arm         mean %   exact     wall/trial  per key   score_iter
-R 1        17.9     4/32      0.23 s       334 us    1544746
-R 4        28.8     7/32      0.61 s       897 us    6158178
-R 16       48.9     13/32     2.26 s      3345 us   24635219
terminal K5 51.1     13/32     0.25 s       368 us    1428322
swept K1    60.6     16/32     0.89 s      1319 us     209393
swept K5    75.3     21/32     0.99 s      1461 us    1164490
swept K10   81.1     23/32     1.13 s      1675 us    2519731
swept K20   81.2     23/32     1.44 s      2136 us    5505752

K=10 IS THE OPERATING POINT.  K=20 buys nothing -- same 23/32, same mean -- for
28% more time.  This refutes the prediction that sweeping wants a high K, which
came from the probe's top-20 recall (35/45 against top-5's 30/45) measured AT THE
TRUE KEY.  In a sweep, raising K helps the true key AND all 675 wrong keys, each
of which also gets K seeds and a better best-of-K score; the extra recall is real
but does not convert into discrimination.

Marginal cost per climb RISES with K -- 35.5, 42.8, 46.1 us across K=1->5->10->20
-- because lower-ranked seeds are less constrained, so their climbs are dearer.
Estimates made before measuring were 1360 us (K=10) and 1810 us (K=20), i.e. low
by 23% and 15%, in the direction that caveat predicted.

Against the baseline: swept K=10 breaks 23/32 against -R 16's 13/32 in HALF the
wall time.  Even K=1 beats -R 16 by 3 breaks at 40% of the cost.

score_iter misleads again: swept K1 has the LOWEST count of any arm (0.21M, 7x
below -R 1) while taking 4x -R 1's wall time.

== K extended to 35 and 50 (same 32 paired trials) ==

arm         mean %   exact     wall/trial  per key   score_iter
-R 16       48.9     13/32     2.11 s      3118 us   24635219
swept K5    75.3     21/32     0.99 s      1457 us    1164490
swept K10   81.1     23/32     1.12 s      1650 us    2519731
swept K20   81.2     23/32     1.43 s      2113 us    5505752
swept K35   81.1     23/32     1.92 s      2838 us   10395636
swept K50   84.3     24/32     2.45 s      3626 us   15613697

The curve is a PLATEAU from K=10 to K=35 -- 23/32 at all three, mean flat at
81.1-81.2 -- and then one extra recovery at K=50 for +120% time over K=10.  So
"K=20 saturates" was too strong: the plateau is real but it is a flat stretch
rather than a ceiling, and pushing 2.5x past it does eventually convert.  What
the plateau does confirm is the mechanism: raising K lifts the wrong keys'
best-of-K alongside the true key's, so gains arrive slowly and unevenly instead
of tracking the true-key recall curve.

23 vs 24 of 32 is ONE TRIAL and is not statistically meaningful on its own.  The
solid part is the shape -- steep to K=10, flat to K=35, no cliff either way --
not the single extra break at 50.

Marginal cost is linear and unremarkable across the whole range, ~46 us per extra
climb.  K=10 remains the operating point: at K=50 you pay -R 16 prices (3626 vs
3118 us per key) for 24/32 against its 13/32 -- still a large win over the
baseline, but a poor deal against K=10's 23/32 at 45% of the cost.

== CORRECTION: the swept ranking figures above were measured with a buggy probe ==

The eval probe's swept enumeration used an EXCLUSIVE upper bound on the
alignment -- `for at in range(1, n-2*L-1)` -- which omits at = n-2L-1.  That is
exactly the TERMINAL alignment, so every message ending in its doubling lost its
true hypothesis.  Corrected (`range(1, n-2*L-1+1)`):

                    reported    corrected
  L>=7 exists       45/48       48/48
  L>=7 IC top-1     56%         65%
  L>=7 IC top-5     67%         79%
  L>=7 IC top-20    78%         92%
  L>=9 exists       27/33       32/33
  L>=9 IC top-1     56%         69%
  L>=9 IC top-5     70%         84%

Recall is PERFECT at L>=7, as it must be: sweeping enumerates a superset of the
terminal alignments, so it cannot have worse recall than the terminal mode's
200/200.  That superset argument is what made 45/48 suspicious enough to chase.

The conclusion "raising the floor to 9 does NOT improve the ranking (56% top-1
either way)" was an artefact of the bug.  It does improve it -- 69% against 65%
top-1, 84% against 79% top-5 -- though on a smaller and different population
(11 of 66 messages against 16).

enigma.cc was NEVER affected: its bound is `at + 2*len + 1 <= textlength`, which
is inclusive.  The closure verification used the corrected enumeration and
matched exactly, and every end-to-end break number (23/32 at K=10, etc.) comes
from the binary, so all of those stand.

== CORRECTION 2: "32/33 at L>=9" was the >=1-CABLE FILTER, not a recall failure ==

The probe scored a trial as having no correct seed unless that seed pinned at
least one CABLE.  Recall of a correct seed is:

                        correct seed exists    ... pinning >=1 cable
  L>=7                  48/48                  48/48
  L>=9                  33/33                  32/33

The one trial is ROMANOVKA, which is flanked by N and G rather than X -- the same
message that broke an early version of this probe.  Its only true variant is
therefore "sep": the separator alone, ONE anchor edge, the weakest menu in the
set.  (The two flank variants assert flanks the plaintext does not have and are
correctly rejected -- 0 survivors each.)

With a single anchor, how far the closure propagates depends on the rotor key.
The same alignment over the three reps:

  rep 1   11 pins,  4 cables
  rep 2    4 pins,  0 cables   <-- the "failure"
  rep 3   12 pins,  5 cables

So it is the expected tail of a weak menu, not a defect in the deduction or the
enumeration.  A 0-cable seed is not worthless either: pinning a letter as
carrying no cable is the --no-plug effect, worth 25 of the 325 candidate toggles,
and the tool pins those findings too.  It is simply much weaker than a seed that
pins cables, which is why the stricter reading is the more useful one when asking
whether a seed will carry a climb.

== RENAME: the options changed name and the position flag INVERTED ==

  --double-length L      ->  --doubling-report L
  --double-z Z           ->  --doubling-z Z
  --double-mismatches N  ->  --doubling-mismatches N
  --signature-seed K     ->  --self-crib-seeds K
  --signature-length L   ->  --self-crib-length L      (default 4 -> 6)
  --signature-sweep      ->  --self-crib-signature     (INVERTED)

"signature" was only true of a doubled word CLOSING the message, and misleading
everywhere else -- ROMANOVKA and NIKOLAJEWO are place names, not sign-offs.  The
observable is a DOUBLING; the deduction that exploits it is a SELF-CRIB.

The position flag now follows --crib/--crib-at: the default assumes nothing
(hypothesise the doubling anywhere) and --self-crib-signature adds the knowledge
that it closes the message.  That also puts the word "signature" only where it
is true.

any L9       50.8    19/40      0.72 s     1065 us    1328

ANYWHERE DOMINATES SIGNATURE AT EVERY LENGTH, and the gap widens as the floor
rises -- 28/40 against 16/40 at L=4, 19/40 against 4/40 at L=9 -- because a long
doubling CLOSING the message is rare (only 4 of the 66 corpus messages have an 8+
terminal one) while long doublings SOMEWHERE stay common.

Every anywhere cell beats -R 16 except L=9, which ties it (19/40) at 2.7x less
time.  On this population the swept self-crib is never worse than a 16-restart
baseline.

SIGNATURE-RESTRICTED IS WORSE THAN -R 16 AT EVERY LENGTH here (best 16/40 at
L=4).  It only wins when the message really does end with its doubling, which on
a realistic mix it usually does not -- which is why it is the flag and not the
default, and why "terminal first, it is cheapest" only holds when you have reason
to believe the message is signed off.

-R 0 and -R 1 are indistinguishable (5/40 both, 18.3 against 17.0 mean, noise on
40 trials).  Both are a SINGLE climb; the kick only changes which basin it starts
from.  Both are ~10x worse than -R 16.

Two knees:
  * cheapest thing that beats -R 16: any L8 -- 22/40 against 19/40 at 1213 us
    per key against 2901, i.e. 2.4x CHEAPER for 3 more breaks;
  * best value overall: any L6 (the shipped default) -- 26/40 at 2428 us per key,
    still under -R 16's cost for 7 more breaks.  L5 and L4 buy 1 and 2 more
    breaks for 1.7x and 3.1x the time.

== the measurement that set --self-crib-length 6 ==

Fixed population: all 20 corpus messages carrying a 4+ doubling ANYWHERE, 40
trials, K=10, 676-key sweeps.

arm            mean %   exact     wall/trial  per key   hypotheses
-R 16          50.5     19/40     1.98 s      2931 us   --
signature L4   46.6     16/40     0.38 s       569 us     20
anywhere L4    76.8     28/40     5.12 s      7573 us   2806
anywhere L5    72.5     27/40     2.86 s      4224 us   2498
anywhere L6    70.5     26/40     1.65 s      2438 us   2196
anywhere L7    65.8     24/40     1.07 s      1588 us   1901
anywhere L9    50.8     19/40     0.71 s      1053 us   1328

Lowering the floor buys breaks MONOTONICALLY (19 -> 28 as L goes 9 -> 4), so the
earlier worry that L=4 would be imprecise was wrong -- it is the best cell on
recovery.  But cost rises faster than the gain: L=7 -> L=4 is 4.8x the time for
+4 breaks of 40.  L=6 is the knee -- just under -R 16's own per-key cost while
breaking 26/40 against its 19/40, and keeping 93% of L=4's breaks for a third of
the time.

This also revises the terminal figure: on THIS population (any doubling, not
only terminal ones) the signature restriction breaks 16/40, BELOW a bare -R 16's
19/40.  Restricting only wins when the message really does end with its
doubling, which is why it is the flag and not the default.
