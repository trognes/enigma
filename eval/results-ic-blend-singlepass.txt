# Can one blended pass replace the staged pre-pass? (PERFORMANCE.md 6.4)
# wehrmacht, L50/70/90, 300 paired trials/length/family, 2 families,
# all arms calibrated to the same score_iter.

## three arms, same 1800 paired instances
   m4a10 (shipped, no blend)     33.9
   a10 + blend lam=30 (one pass) 35.3
   m4a10 + blend lam=30 (both)   38.3

   one-pass  vs shipped   +1.4pp [-0.4,+3.2]   tie
   both      vs shipped   +4.4pp [+3.1,+5.6]
   both      vs one-pass  +3.0pp [+1.3,+4.7]   staging still earns its place

## restarts earned (the reason one-pass is even competitive)
   staged m4a10 calibrated to R=84-87; single-pass a10 to R=146-148.
   Dropping the pre-pass is ~0.59x the cost (section 6.10), so the single
   pass buys ~1.7x the restarts at matched compute -- and that is exactly
   enough to offset losing the pre-pass, but not enough to beat keeping it.

## reading
   YES, a single blended pass can replace staging: it matches the shipped
   recipe (tie, nominally +1.4pp) while being simpler and dropping a knob
   whose best value is register-dependent (m4a10 telegraphic / i4a10 german
   prose, section 6.10 follow-up).
   NO, it should not: keeping both is +3.0pp over the single pass. The
   pre-pass and the blend are COMPLEMENTARY, not redundant -- consistent
   with them acting by different mechanisms (pre-pass = basin selection on a
   smoother surface; blend = gradient shaping on the target surface).
