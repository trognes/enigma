# ngrams-telegraphic — telegraphic German n-gram tables

Domain-matched German scoring tables for **real Wehrmacht Enigma traffic**, which is
telegraphic (`X` = word separator ≈ 7 %, `Q` for *ch*, spelled-out numbers `EINS ZWO
DREI …`) and looks nothing like prose German (`X` ≈ 0.07 %). The bundled prose tables in
`../ngrams/` penalise the true telegraphic plaintext, so on short/garbled real messages a
spurious plugboard can outscore the truth; these tables fix that.

## Use

```sh
./enigma -d ngrams-telegraphic -l german -a -c -S m4a10 -J --gainfix-best3 -R 200 -T4 < cipher.txt
```

Just point `-d` at this directory; everything else is the normal recommended recipe.

## When to use it — and when NOT to

- **Use for authentic telegraphic Wehrmacht traffic** (the real thing: operational orders,
  `MELDUNG`/`BETRIEBSTOFF`/`MUNITION`, phonetic alphabet, `X` separators).
- **Do NOT use for prose German**, or for the synthetic `make crackquality` benchmark
  (random prose excerpts) — there the bundled `../ngrams/` tables are correct. These tables
  deliberately bake in telegraphic orthography, so on prose they are a domain *mis*match
  (the inverse of the win below).

## How they were built & measured

Generated by `eval/build_telegraphic_ngrams.py` — the bundled prose German tables bent
toward the **published telegraphic statistics** of Sullivan & Weierud, *"Breaking German
Army Ciphers"* (Cryptologia 2005), Appendix C: Fig 17 (single-letter frequencies) and
Fig 18 (top-400 trigrams), over ~20 000 letters of 1941 Enigma decrypts. Marginal-matching
strength A=0.5 (monogram) / B=2.0 (trigram), the net-best over the held-out set.

The **original published Appendix-C tables** are committed here verbatim as reference data
(the primary source, unmodified — not the derived `german_*.txt` scoring tables):
`appendix-c-fig17-monograms.txt` (26 letters, %) and `appendix-c-fig18-trigrams.txt`
(top-400 trigrams, counts per ~20 000 letters). The generator regenerates them too.

Held-out validation (`eval/eval_telegraphic.py`, the 69 authentic messages in `eval/`,
rotor key fixed, plugboard hidden and hill-climbed, mean %-of-letters correct):

| length band | n | prose | telegraphic |
|---|---|---|---|
| 40–69 | 14 | 15.3 | 24.3 |
| 70–119 | 20 | 28.8 | **67.5** |
| ≥120 | 24 | 75.5 | 95.4 |
| **all** | 69 | **39.3** | **60.2** |

+20.9 pp mean, winning 36 messages / losing 12. Biggest gains in the 70–119-letter band,
above the ~23-letter unicity distance but short enough that prose can't rank the truth.

**Honesty caveat:** Appendix C is aggregate over ~100 decrypts of the *same* HG Nord traffic
family the 69 come from, so this measures a domain-matched table on in-domain real traffic —
not a claim about arbitrary German. No individual message's plaintext went into the tables.

Regenerate with `python3 eval/build_telegraphic_ngrams.py` (env `A=`/`B=` to retune).
